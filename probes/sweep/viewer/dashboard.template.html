Living-room sweep

A wheeled two-arm robot fetches a brush and a bin, then gets the trash, and only the trash, from a coffee table and a sofa into the bin. It sees the room through its cameras; nobody tells it where things are or which of them are trash.

MuJoCo 4.0.1, real contact physics PAL TIAGo++ from MuJoCo Menagerie score from the final physical state recorded runs: see the comparison page

Environment

What the robot sees

Standard setting, as in the Embodied First Exam: the head camera in colour and depth with its calibration, two wrist cameras in colour, and the robot's own joint readings. No object positions, no labels, no count of the trash, and no tool that turns a pixel into a position: that geometry is the agent's to do. The privileged setting adds the position and kind of every object; it exists for ablation.

Sweeping is contact physics: the bristles push whatever is in front of them, and a piece falls into the bin only if the bin is under the edge it leaves. Taking a tool is assisted: it attaches when the hand closes on the handle.

      

    

    

      
The task prompt
probes/sweep/instruction.md, verbatim: Harbor hands it to the agent as its task, with nothing added

      

    

    
Episode limits: 900 s of robot time; the world is paused between calls; one attempt, no resets. How an end state is scored is the next section.

  


  

    
Reward design

    

      
reward = (trash in the bin ÷ all trash) × 0.5mistakes

      

        
Trash in the bin
A piece counts when its centre lies between the bin's four walls, above the bin's floor and at most 3 cm above the rim, with the bin upright and its opening up. Trash under the bin, or a bin set down over it, does not count.

        
Mistake
A personal item that ends in the bin, a personal item that ends on the floor, or the bin on its side. Each one halves the reward.

        
Earns nothing
Trash on the floor or anywhere else outside the bin (it costs nothing either). Reaching the tools, picking them up, touching things: those moments are logged for the failure analysis and paid nothing.

        
When it is measured
Once, at the end: when the agent stops or its 900 s of robot time are used up, the room is left alone for 2 s and the scorer reads where everything lies in the simulator. One number from 0 to 1 goes to /logs/verifier/reward.txt.

        
Why this shape
The first factor pays for progress on the task; the second makes care multiply rather than add, so sweeping everything off the table, personal items included, scores far below doing the job properly. Doing nothing scores 0.
